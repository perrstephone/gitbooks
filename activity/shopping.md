# 🛍️ Shopping Districts

## Wicker Park

Near North West of the city, there's an up-and-coming/hipster district that includes a lot of good food and shopping at much better prices than downtown. These are largely boutique stores and thrift shops. I'd advise taking the subway out, exit at the Division stop, and head NW on Milwaukee or W on Division.&#x20;

* Ragstock: Thrift store with lots of flannel.
* Belmont Army: Surplus shop with some actually interesting surplus.
* Mildblend Supply: High end denim.
* Geology Rocks! And Minerals: Good place to look at and buy rocks.
* Secret Agent Supply&#x20;
* Myopic Books
* store B vintage: Vintage boutique.&#x20;

## Mag Mile (Michigan Ave)

This is the premier shopping district of Chicago.&#x20;

* Tribune Tower: Embedded in the former home of the Chicago Tribune are stones from various famous places, such as the Colosseum, the Berlin Wall, and more. Sounds weird, but it's a fun way to spend 20 minutes.
* Wrigley Building: Iconic building housing a beautiful walkway, a Ghirardelli store, and a Lou Malnati's.&#x20;
* Water Tower Place: Large mall with several iconic stores, such as the American Girl store.
* The Chicago Water Tower: One of the few remaining buildings that survived the Great Chicago Fire in the 1800s.&#x20;
* Luxury flagship stores: Saks Fifth Avenue, Tiffany's and Co., Cartier
* Luxury co-signment stores: The RealReal
* Traditional flagship stores: Apple, Under Armor, Nike, Sephora, etc.&#x20;

## State Street

If Mag Mile is so good, why isn't there a Mag Mile 2? Well that's what State Street is, more or less. After crossing into the Loop from the north, State turns into another, more low key, entertainment and shopping center.&#x20;

* Chicago Theatre: Iconic sign, sometimes they have interesting acts.
* Stores: Uniqlo, Macy's, Crocs, Zara, Dick Blick's&#x20;
* The Goth Target: It's a normal Target, but the outside has gothic architectural elements.

## Gold Coast

If Mag Mile 2 is so good, then why isn't there a Mag Mile 3? Well, there is at the north end of Mag Mile and it is the most expensive part of Chicago. Also known as Viagra Triangle, it's not all eye watering prices, but there are more boutiques, jewelers, and luxury stores than anywhere else. The namesake comes from the prevalence of old men with remarkably expensive sports cars.&#x20;

* Luxury stores: Hermés, Prada, Chanel, Arc'teryx
* The Original Playboy Mansion
* Upscale restaurants: Carmine's, Le Colonial
* Newberry Library

## Fulton Market

Fulton Market is a food district that is lined with restaurants and bars. These restaurants range in price, but it is a good area for bar hopping.

* Kuma's Corner: A metal themed bar/restaurant.
* Emporium: A bar that doubles as an arcade.
